# Design a Project from Scratch, Then Go Find Somewhere to Build It

## 1. Goal

Lesson 06 gave you three ways to come up with project material. This lesson runs the third one, the hard case: nothing on your resume lines up with the job you want, so the posting itself becomes the starting point and you reason backward from it to a project worth building.

Here's the whole idea in one sentence. The six-stage chain from 07 still applies, start to finish, and the only thing that changes is stage 3, where mini-project-design flips from elevate mode to from-scratch mode. There's no second pipeline to learn. There's a fork to recognize, and one rule that 08 adds: if you can't say where the work will get done, you don't have a plan, you have a wish.

You won't be done when you finish reading. You'll pick a posting you actually care about, run the design yourself, end up with a forward-looking case draft, and face the question 07 lets you skip. Where, specifically, are you going to go do this?

---

## 2. What to Do

1. Start from 07-elevate-existing-project. Almost everything here builds on it, so if any of it feels hazy, go back and reread before you keep going.
2. Read the README straight through, then circle back to three parts: the workflow comparison in section 2, the design to execution timeline in section 6, and the wrap-up covering 06, 07, and 08 in section 9.
3. Pull up John's Pulse example and put the forward-looking case-cn.md next to executed-case-cn.md, the version rewritten after the internship was over. Find three things that changed in tone and three that changed in substance.
4. Shut the tutorial and say it back in your own words: where does each of the three methods start, and where do all three converge into the same downstream pipeline? Then reopen it and see what you got wrong.
5. Pick a job you actually want and copy the entire posting into your own directory. Not the link. Postings come down, and you'll want the text six months from now.
6. Be blunt about where you stand. Write out 5 to 9 gaps between you today and what that posting expects, and flag the ones that are Core. Don't round anything up in your favor.
7. Draft the case for that job in from-scratch mode. Keep it in the future tense the whole way through: what you're going to build, what you're going to learn. Nothing you haven't done yet gets described as finished.
8. Save the hardest question for last. Where would this project actually get built? Name the place and write it down. A matching internship counts. So does six months of real contributions to an open source project. So does an apprenticeship. If nothing on that list is available to you, shrink the project until it's something you could pull off alone, in six months, with no one guiding you.

**Estimated time:** 90 to 150 minutes, or about 30 if you're just reading and skipping the design work in steps 5 through 8

## 3. Checklist

- [ ] **You can name the fork**: you can explain that the six-stage chains in 07 and 08 are identical except at stage 3, and describe what goes into elevate mode and from-scratch mode and what each one gives back.
- [ ] **You read both versions**: you compared the forward-looking case against the executed one and can name one concrete shift in tone and one in substance.
- [ ] **You can walk the timeline**: you can follow the case file from January to September 2026, from nothing at all, to a design doc, to interview material, to the grown-up version.
- [ ] **The venue rule sank in**: you can say why a design doc without a place to execute it is a wish, and name the question 08 asks in review that never comes up in 07.
- [ ] **The triangle makes sense**: you can lay out where the mentor-designed, elevate, and from-scratch routes each begin, and why all three end up in the same downstream pipeline.
- [ ] **You have a target posting**: the full text of a job you actually want is sitting in your own directory, not a link that will 404 by next month.
- [ ] **The gaps are written down**: 5 to 9 of them against that posting, Core ones flagged, and the list stings a little to read.
- [ ] **The draft exists and stays honest**: every line of it is something you intend to do, not something you're quietly implying you've already done.
- [ ] **The venue is on paper**: you've named where this project gets built, or you've already cut it down to something you could finish alone in six months.
