# learn_system_design-project

System design is the skill of taking a vague, open-ended prompt and pushing it into a concrete plan that survives real traffic. Production problems are never clean. Requirements are fuzzy and keep moving, the thing you are changing is usually already running, and every decision is squeezed by cost, deadlines, and the size of the team. Interviews carve system design into its own round because it exposes judgment in a way an algorithm question never will. It is the kind of skill that separates nobody in the short run and sets your ceiling in the long run.

This course turns that into muscle memory. One reusable line of attack runs from clarifying scope to putting a full architecture on the board. Ten problems of rising difficulty walk that line end to end. The last stretch pulls it together into a method you can use in interviews and in design reviews at work. By the end you can take a problem you have never seen and drive it yourself: clarify the scope, model the data before you reach for components, and back your tradeoffs with reasons that survive pushback. The depth is pitched at senior and staff interviews and real design reviews, not at a first look at the topic.

Nothing here is an answer key to memorize. Each problem is staged as a real interview, dialogue between interviewer and candidate with narration explaining what is happening underneath, and there is an agent you can practice against the moment you finish reading. Technology choices are standardized on AWS and mainstream open source, and the examples are consumer products anyone in North America will recognize. Swap the stack and the judgment still holds.
